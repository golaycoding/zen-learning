## Rule Review

### Apostrophes

An apostrophe never makes a word plural — only -s or -es does that.

- Incorrect: two car's Correct: two cars

An apostrophe + s shows that something belongs to a person or thing (possession).

- Jack's backpack is red. (the backpack belongs to Jack)

An apostrophe also stands in for missing letters in a contraction (two words joined into one).

- we're = we are it's = it is don't = do not

Tip: if you can replace the word with "it is" or "we are," it needs an apostrophe. If you are just talking about more than one of something, it does not.

### Commas with FANBOYS

FANBOYS stands for the seven coordinating conjunctions: for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so. When one of these words joins two complete sentences (each half could stand alone), place a comma before the conjunction.

- I wanted to leave, but the movie was not over yet.
- She practiced every day, so she made the team.

Tip: cover up the FANBOYS word and check both halves. If each half is a complete sentence on its own, the comma belongs right before the conjunction.

### Semicolons

A semicolon joins two closely related complete sentences without using a conjunction. Each side of the semicolon must be able to stand alone as its own sentence.

- Karen is a teacher; Susie is a nurse.

Two common mistakes: leaving out all punctuation between two complete sentences, and using both a semicolon and a conjunction (such as "and") together — only one or the other is needed.
