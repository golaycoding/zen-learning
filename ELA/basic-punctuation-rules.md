# Basic Punctuation Rules
### A Grade 7 Reference Guide, based on the Sparkle English YouTube lesson "13 Basic Punctuation Rules"

Punctuation gives structure to written English. These 13 rules cover the essentials every writer needs before moving on to more advanced topics.

YouTube: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zVu-XvULZNg
---

## 1. End Every Statement with a Period

A declarative sentence — a sentence that makes a statement — always ends with a period (also called a full stop).

- *She loves chocolate.*
- *He is my best friend.*
- *We have a meeting in two hours.*

## 2. Capitalize the First Word of a Sentence

The first letter of every sentence must be capitalized. After a sentence ends (with a period, question mark, or exclamation mark), leave one space and begin the next sentence with a capital letter.

- *She loves chocolate. He loves pizza.*

## 3. Capitalize Proper Nouns

A common noun names a general category (boy, day, month, country, city, ocean). A proper noun names something specific within that category (David, Friday, July, Canada, New York, Atlantic Ocean) and must always be capitalized.

- *His name is David.*
- *The best day of the week is Friday.*
- *My favorite month is July.*

## 4. Never Use an Apostrophe to Make a Word Plural

Apostrophes do not create plurals — only an *s* (or *es*) does.

- **Incorrect:** *They have two car's.*
- **Correct:** *They have two cars.*

## 5. Use Apostrophes for Contractions and Possession

Apostrophes serve two purposes: joining two words into a contraction, or showing that something belongs to someone.

- **Contraction:** *don't* (do not), *you're* (you are), *he's* (he is)
- **Possession:** *Jack's new car is beautiful.* (the car belongs to Jack)

## 6. End Every Question with a Question Mark

Any interrogative sentence — a sentence that asks something — must end with a question mark.

- *Do you like pizza?*
- *Should we go to the cinema tonight?*

## 7. Use Exclamation Marks for Strong Emotion

An exclamation mark signals excitement, strong feeling, or urgency. It can express joy, anger, or a warning, and is common in commands.

- *Wow, you look beautiful!*
- *Watch out!*

## 8. Use Commas to Separate Items in a List of Three or More

Two items don't need a comma between them, but a list of three or more does. The comma placed right before the final conjunction (and/or) is called the Oxford comma, and using it is generally recommended for clarity.

- *I bought coffee, tea, and milk.*
- *Jake has been to India, Pakistan, and China.*

## 9. Use a Comma Before FANBOYS When Joining Two Complete Sentences

When two independent clauses (complete sentences that could stand alone) are joined by a coordinating conjunction, place a comma before the conjunction. The seven coordinating conjunctions are remembered with the acronym **FANBOYS**: for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so.

- *I want to go out tonight, but I need to study.*
- *He is a doctor, and she is a lawyer.*

## 10. Use a Semicolon to Join Closely Related Independent Clauses

A semicolon can connect two complete sentences that are closely related in meaning, without using a conjunction. (The same idea could also be written as two separate sentences, or joined with a comma and a conjunction.)

- *Karen is a teacher; Susie is a nurse.*
- *Luigi doesn't like seafood; he prefers pasta.*

## 11. Use a Colon to Introduce a List

A colon connects a complete independent clause to a list that follows it.

- *They serve many types of food: Chinese, Indian, and American.*
- *There are three things every child needs: education, food, and family.*

## 12. Use a Hyphen to Join Words That Form a Single Adjective

When two or more words combine to describe a noun as a single adjective, join them with a hyphen.

- *well-known author*
- *brand-new television*
- *chocolate-covered peanuts*

## 13. Use a Hyphen with Compound Numbers Between 21 and 99

Numbers from twenty-one through ninety-nine are hyphenated when written out. (A round number like "twenty" does not need a hyphen since it's a single word.)

- *twenty-one*
- *eighty-three*
- *She is thirty-two years old.*
