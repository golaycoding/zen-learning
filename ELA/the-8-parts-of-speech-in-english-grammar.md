# The Eight Parts of Speech

### Grade 7 Grammar Study Guide

Every word in a sentence plays a specific grammatical role. Grammarians sort these roles into eight categories called the parts of speech. Knowing them builds a foundation for reading comprehension and strong writing.

## Resource: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=chjmnCSPnbw

## 1. Nouns

A noun names a person, place, thing, or idea. In a sentence, a noun can function as the subject, the direct object, the indirect object, and other roles.

**Types of nouns:**

- **People:** girl, teacher, dad
- **Places:** mountain, kitchen, Spain
- **Things or concepts:** love, knowledge, rugby

## 2. Pronouns

A pronoun replaces a noun so a sentence does not sound repetitive. Compare these two versions:

> Repetitive: "William is my fiancé. William is tall. William told me William wants to marry me."
>
> Natural: "William is my fiancé. He is tall. He told me he wants to marry me."

**Common types of pronouns:**

- **Personal:** I, you, she, he, they, us
- **Demonstrative:** this, that, these, those
- **Indefinite:** everybody, anybody, one, other
- **Interrogative:** who, what, which, whose
- **Reflexive:** myself, yourself, themselves
- **Relative:** who, which, that

## 3. Adjectives

An adjective describes a noun. It can appear directly before the noun ("my orange cat") or after a linking verb ("my cat is orange").

One useful rule is adjective order. Native speakers arrange multiple adjectives in a set sequence, even though this rule is rarely taught directly in school:

1. **Opinion** (beautiful, ugly)
2. **Size** (big, tall)
3. **Physical quality** (neat, rough)
4. **Shape** (round, square)
5. **Age** (old, new)
6. **Color** (pink, orange)
7. **Origin** (Japanese, Egyptian)
8. **Material** (leather, plastic)
9. **Type** (four-legged, unisex)
10. **Purpose** (cooking, sewing)

_Example: "my beautiful brown leather suitcase" follows this order — opinion, then color, then material._

## 4. Verbs

A verb expresses an action or a state of being.

- **Dynamic (action) verbs:** describe physical actions, such as run, sing, walk
- **Stative verbs:** describe feelings or states, such as love, think, feel
- **Auxiliary (helping) verbs:** be, have, and do change the tense, mood, or voice of another verb
- **Modal verbs:** can, could, may, might, must, should, will, would, shall, ought express possibility, necessity, or ability
- **Phrasal verbs:** combine a verb with a preposition to form a single meaning, such as "give up" or "look after"

## 5. Adverbs

An adverb describes a verb, an adjective, another adverb, or an entire sentence.

- _"I gently lifted him out of bed."_ — describes the verb "lifted"
- _"He is unusually tall."_ — describes the adjective "tall"
- _"Unfortunately, we had already left."_ — describes the whole sentence

## 6. Prepositions

A preposition shows the relationship between words in a sentence, often indicating time or place.

_Examples: on the table, beside the wall, on Monday_

A common myth claims a sentence should never end with a preposition. In casual, natural English, this is not a strict rule — "Where do you come from?" sounds far more natural than the stiff alternative "From where do you come?" In formal writing, it is still often better to avoid ending a sentence with a preposition.

## 7. Conjunctions

A conjunction connects words, phrases, or clauses.

- **Coordinating conjunctions** join elements of equal importance. The seven are remembered with the acronym FANBOYS: for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so.
  _Example: "I had a terrible headache, but I still went to school."_
- **Correlative conjunctions** work in pairs, such as either/or and neither/nor.
  _Example: "I want either the beef or the turkey."_
- **Subordinating conjunctions** connect a dependent clause to an independent clause, such as because, since, although, while, whereas.
  _Example: "I ate dinner because I was hungry."_

## 8. Articles

Articles specify whether a noun is general or specific.

- **The definite article, "the,"** refers to one particular thing both speaker and listener already know about: "Give me the ticket."
- **The indefinite articles, "a" and "an,"** refer to something general, not a specific item: "Can you pass me an apple?" Use "a" before consonant sounds and "an" before vowel sounds.

---

## Quick Reference Summary

- **Noun:** names a person, place, thing, or idea
- **Pronoun:** replaces a noun
- **Adjective:** describes a noun
- **Verb:** expresses an action or state of being
- **Adverb:** describes a verb, adjective, another adverb, or a sentence
- **Preposition:** shows the relationship between words, often time or place
- **Conjunction:** connects words, phrases, or clauses
- **Article:** signals whether a noun is general ("a," "an") or specific ("the")

## Practice

Look around the room and write three sentences describing three different things you can see. Use at least two adjectives in the correct order (opinion, size, physical quality, shape, age, color, origin, material, type, purpose) in each sentence.

Write your sentences on a separate sheet of paper.
